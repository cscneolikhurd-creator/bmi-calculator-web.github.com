      Frequently Asked Questions

      What are the best Indian fermented foods for gut health?

      Idli, dosa, dhokla, curd (dahi), buttermilk (chaas), kanji (fermented carrot drink), achaar (traditional pickles), and appam are excellent Indian probiotic foods. They contain live cultures of Lactobacillus and other beneficial bacteria. Idli/dosa batter fermentation increases B vitamins and improves digestibility. Aim for at least one serving daily.

      Why is H. pylori prevalence so high in India?

      H. pylori infects 60–70% of Indians (vs 30% in the US). It spreads through contaminated food/water and poor sanitation. Most are asymptomatic, but it can cause gastritis, ulcers, and increases stomach cancer risk. If you have persistent bloating, burning, or upper abdominal pain, ask your doctor for an H. pylori test (stool antigen or urea breath test).

      How much fiber should Indians eat for gut health?

      ICMR-NIN 2020 recommends 40g fiber/day for Indian adults (higher than USDA's 30g) due to high-carb diets and higher risk of metabolic disease. Best Indian fiber sources: ragi, bajra, jowar, whole dal, isabgol (psyllium husk), guava, amla, leafy greens (palak, methi), and sabzi. Target 25–30g minimum; ideal 40g.

      Are traditional Indian gut remedies effective?

      Several have evidence: jeera (cumin) water stimulates digestion and may reduce bloating; ajwain (carom seeds) has carminative effects; saunf (fennel) soothes gas; hing (asafoetida) helps with bloating and IBS; buttermilk (chaas) with roasted jeera aids digestion; triphala (Ayurvedic blend) has mild laxative and prebiotic effects. Do not replace medical treatment — use as supportive.

      What is the gut microbiome?

      The gut microbiome is the collection of trillions of bacteria, fungi, and other microbes living in your digestive tract. These microbes help digest food, produce vitamins, train your immune system, and influence mood, metabolism, and overall health.

      How is gut microbiome health measured?

      True measurement requires a stool sample analyzed by labs (16S rRNA sequencing). Our tool uses a validated questionnaire-based approach inspired by the American Gut Project — assessing diet, lifestyle, and symptoms that correlate strongly with microbiome diversity.

      Do probiotics actually work?

      Research shows mixed results. Fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi, idli, dhokla) have stronger evidence than probiotic supplements. The American Gut Project found that diversity of plants in your diet matters more than any single probiotic. Supplements may help after antibiotics or for specific conditions.

      How long does it take to improve gut health?

      Initial changes can appear within 1–2 weeks (bloating, digestion). Significant microbiome shifts take 4–12 weeks of consistent dietary change. Long-term diversity improvements require months of plant-rich eating and lifestyle habits.

      Medical Disclaimer

      This gut microbiome health score is for educational and informational purposes only. It is based on a questionnaire-inspired approach from the American Gut Project (Knight lab, UC San Diego) and does not replace stool sample analysis (16S rRNA sequencing or metagenomics).

      The score is not a diagnostic tool. If you experience persistent digestive symptoms (bloating, pain, blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, chronic diarrhea), consult a gastroenterologist. This tool is not appropriate for managing IBS, IBD (Crohn's/ulcerative colitis), celiac disease, SIBO, or other diagnosed gastrointestinal conditions.

      H. pylori note: 60–70% of Indians carry H. pylori. If you have persistent burning, bloating, or upper abdominal pain, ask your doctor for H. pylori testing (stool antigen or urea breath test). Do not self-medicate.

      Individual microbiomes vary significantly based on genetics, geography, medications, and medical history. Avoid making major dietary changes during pregnancy or with chronic illness without medical guidance.
