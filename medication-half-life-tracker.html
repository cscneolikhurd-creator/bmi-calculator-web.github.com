Frequently Asked Questions

What is medication half-life?

Half-life is the time it takes for the concentration of a drug in your body to reduce by 50%. It helps determine dosing frequency and how long a medication stays in your system.

How long until a drug is eliminated?

Generally, it takes 4-5 half-lives for a drug to be considered eliminated (95-97% cleared). Our calculator shows the exact time for your specific medication.

What is steady state concentration?

Steady state is when drug intake equals elimination, typically reached after 4-5 half-lives of consistent dosing. Our multi-dose mode calculates this for you.

What factors affect half-life?

Half-life is affected by: liver function, kidney function, age, weight, genetics, drug interactions, and disease states. Always follow your doctor's dosing instructions.

Can I use this to adjust my medication?

NO. This tool is for educational understanding only. Never adjust your medication dose or schedule based on this calculator. Always follow your prescriber's instructions and consult your pharmacist for any questions.

Is this calculator accurate?

This tool uses first-order elimination kinetics, which applies to most drugs. However, some drugs follow zero-order kinetics (e.g., alcohol, phenytoin at high doses) and real-world blood levels depend on many individual factors. Use for education only.

Medical Disclaimer

This medication half-life tracker is for educational and informational purposes only. It uses first-order elimination kinetics, which applies to most drugs — but not all. Some drugs follow zero-order kinetics (e.g., alcohol, phenytoin at high doses) or have active metabolites with different half-lives.

Never use this tool to: adjust your medication dose, skip doses, or make any treatment decisions. Always follow your prescriber's instructions and consult your pharmacist or physician for medication questions.

Individual half-life varies significantly based on: liver and kidney function, age, weight, genetics, drug interactions, food, and disease states. If you experience side effects or adverse reactions, contact your healthcare provider immediately.

If you suspect an overdose, call emergency services (911 in US, 999 in UK, 112 in EU) or your local poison control center immediately.
