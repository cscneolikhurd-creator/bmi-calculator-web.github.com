Frequently Asked Questions

What is VO2 Max?

VO2 Max (maximal oxygen uptake) is the maximum rate at which your body can consume oxygen during intense exercise. Measured in mL/kg/min, it's considered the gold standard for cardiovascular fitness. Higher VO2 Max correlates with lower risk of heart disease and all-cause mortality.

What is a good VO2 Max?

For men: 35-42 (Fair), 42-48 (Average), 48-54 (Good), 54-60 (Excellent), 60+ (Athlete). For women: 29-35 (Fair), 35-40 (Average), 40-46 (Good), 46-52 (Excellent), 52+ (Athlete). Values vary with age.

How accurate is the Resting HR method?

The Resting HR method is a rough estimate (±10-15%). It uses your maximum heart rate and resting heart rate ratio. The Rockport 1-mile walk test is more accurate (±5-10%) because it accounts for actual performance.

How can I improve my VO2 Max?

Train 3-5x weekly with a mix of: Zone 2 steady cardio (60-70% HRmax) for aerobic base, HIIT intervals (85-95% HRmax) for VO2 improvement, and tempo runs at threshold pace. Increase training volume gradually by 10% per week.

What is the Rockport 1-Mile Walk Test?

The Rockport Fitness Walking Test estimates VO2 Max from walking 1 mile as fast as possible, then measuring heart rate. The formula uses walk time, heart rate, age, gender, and body weight. It's validated for healthy adults.

Does VO2 Max predict longevity?

Yes. Multiple studies (Mandsager et al. 2018, JAMA) show VO2 Max is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality — stronger than smoking, diabetes, or hypertension. Every 1 MET (3.5 mL/kg/min) increase reduces mortality risk by 10-15%.

Medical Disclaimer

This VO2 Max calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Both methods (Resting HR and Rockport 1-Mile Walk) are field estimates with accuracy of ±5-15% compared to lab-based cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET).

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, cardiac stress testing, or a cardiopulmonary exercise test. If you have heart disease, hypertension, diabetes, or joint problems, consult your physician before performing the walk test or starting high-intensity exercise.

Stop exercising immediately if you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or fainting. Call emergency services if symptoms persist.
