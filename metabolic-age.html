Frequently Asked Questions

What is metabolic age?

Metabolic age compares your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) to the average BMR of people your chronological age. If your metabolic age is lower than your actual age, your metabolism is more efficient than average — indicating better fitness, muscle mass, and metabolic health.

How accurate is metabolic age?

Metabolic age is a motivational estimate, not a clinical measurement. It's based on BMR formulas (Mifflin-St Jeor) compared against population averages. Individual metabolism varies widely due to genetics, thyroid function, muscle mass, and medication. Use it as a trend indicator, not a diagnosis.

Can I lower my metabolic age?

Yes. Strength training (2-3x/week) increases muscle mass, which raises BMR. Eating adequate protein (1.6-2.2g/kg), getting 7-9 hours of sleep, staying hydrated, and managing stress all support a healthy metabolism. Results typically show in 8-12 weeks.

Does metabolic age matter for weight loss?

Indirectly. A higher BMR (lower metabolic age) means you burn more calories at rest — making weight loss easier. But total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), diet quality, and activity level matter more than metabolic age itself. Focus on sustainable habits, not the number.

What is a good metabolic health score?

Our score ranges from 0-100: 80+ is excellent (metabolic age below actual age), 60-79 good, 40-59 fair, and below 40 needs attention (metabolic age above actual age). Focus on trends over time rather than the exact number.

Can medication affect metabolic age?

Yes. Thyroid medications, beta-blockers, antidepressants, and steroids can significantly affect BMR. If you're on medication that impacts metabolism, interpret results with caution and consult your doctor. Metabolic age is not diagnostic.

Medical Disclaimer

This metabolic age calculator is for educational and motivational purposes only. Metabolic age is not a clinical measurement — it's an estimate based on BMR formulas (Mifflin-St Jeor) compared against population averages. Individual metabolic rate varies significantly based on genetics, thyroid function, muscle mass, medications, and health conditions.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, thyroid testing, or metabolic assessment. If you experience unexplained weight changes, fatigue, or suspect a metabolic disorder (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism), consult an endocrinologist.

Do not change medications based on this tool's results. Always consult your healthcare provider before making health decisions.

Sources: Mifflin MD et al. (1990) A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure. Am J Clin Nutr. | Frankenfield D et al. (2005) Comparison of predictive equations for resting metabolic rate. J Am Diet Assoc. | Institute of Medicine Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy.
