Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Ponderal Index?

The Ponderal Index (PI), also called Rohrer's Index, measures body leanness using cubic height scaling: PI = weight (kg) / height³ (m³). Unlike BMI, which uses square scaling, PI matches the volume of the human body more accurately, making it fairer for very tall or very short individuals.

What is a normal Ponderal Index?

For adults, a normal Ponderal Index is approximately 10-14 kg/m³. Below 10 suggests underweight; 14-17 suggests overweight; above 17 suggests obesity. For newborns, normal PI is 20-25 kg/m³.

Is Ponderal Index better than BMI?

PI is more accurate for individuals at height extremes. BMI overestimates obesity in tall people and underestimates it in short people because it uses square scaling. PI's cubic scaling better represents actual body volume. However, BMI has more research and clinical reference data.

What is Rohrer's Index?

Rohrer's Index is the original 1908 formula for Ponderal Index: (weight in grams) / (height in cm)³ × 100. It's used primarily in pediatric medicine to assess newborns and children.

What is the Corpulence Index?

The Corpulence Index (CI) is essentially the same as Ponderal Index — weight divided by height cubed. Some sources use the term interchangeably. It was historically used in insurance and military screening.

Can Ponderal Index be used for children?

Yes. PI is widely used in pediatric medicine to assess newborns for macrosomia and to track growth. Normal newborn PI is 20-25; the value decreases with age. Always consult a pediatrician for interpretation.

Medical Disclaimer

This Ponderal Index calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. PI values are estimates based on the Rohrer formula and do not account for individual factors like muscle mass, bone density, ethnicity, or body composition.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Athletes, pregnant women, elderly individuals, and children may have healthy PI values outside standard ranges. Consult a physician or registered dietitian for personalized assessment.
