Frequently Asked Questions

What is a normal Ponderal Index for Indians?

For Indians, normal PI depends on height and Asian BMI cutoffs (BMI 23 = overweight, 25 = obese). For a 165cm Indian, normal PI ≈ 10–13.9; for 175cm, ≈ 10–13.1; for 185cm, ≈ 10–12.4. Our calculator shows personalized cutoffs based on your height.

Why do PI cutoffs change with height?

Because PI = BMI / height(m). The same BMI gives a different PI at different heights. At BMI 23: a 155cm person has PI 14.8, a 185cm person has PI 12.4. This is why fixed 10/14/17 thresholds are only accurate for a specific height range (~165–170cm).

Why does my PI say 'obese' but BMI says 'normal'?

This is exactly what PI was designed for. If you are shorter than average, BMI underestimates your body fat while PI gives a clearer picture. If they disagree, get a body fat % measurement (DEXA, InBody, or our body fat calculator) for accuracy. Athletic build is a common reason for high PI but low fat.

Is PI useful for athletes?

Not ideal. Muscular athletes have high weight-for-height, so PI (like BMI) misclassifies many athletes as obese. For athletes, use FFMI (Fat-Free Mass Index) and body fat % instead. PI is best for general population screening, especially for those at height extremes.

What is a normal PI for newborns and children?

Pediatric PI is age-specific: newborn 20–25, 1 year 15–18, 5 years 13–15, 10 years 12–14, 15 years 11–14. Values drop as children grow taller faster than they gain weight. Always consult a pediatrician — premature, IUGR, and macrosomic babies have distinct PI patterns.

What is the Ponderal Index formula?

PI = weight (kg) / height³ (m³). Example: 70kg at 175cm = 70 / (1.75)³ = 70 / 5.36 = 13.1 kg/m³. Normal adult: 10–14 (varies with height and region).

What is Rohrer's Index?

Rohrer's Index is the original 1908 formula: (weight in g / height in cm³) × 100. Mathematically similar to PI but uses different units. Historically used in pediatric medicine and military screening.

Is PI better than BMI?

PI is more accurate for individuals at height extremes. BMI overestimates obesity in tall people and underestimates it in short people. However, PI still cannot distinguish muscle from fat. BMI has more clinical research. PI is a supplementary metric, not a replacement.

Medical Disclaimer

This Ponderal Index calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. PI values are estimates based on the Rohrer formula and do not account for individual factors like muscle mass, bone density, ethnicity, or body composition.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Athletes, pregnant women, elderly individuals, and children may have healthy PI values outside standard ranges. If PI and BMI disagree, get a body fat measurement for clarity. Consult a physician or registered dietitian for personalized assessment.
