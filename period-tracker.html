Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is a period tracker for Indian women with PCOS?

PCOS affects 1 in 5 Indian women and causes irregular ovulation, making calendar-based prediction unreliable. Use OPK (ovulation predictor kits) or consult a gynecologist for follicular tracking ultrasound. Lifestyle changes (5-10% weight loss, strength training, low-GI diet with millets) can restore regular cycles in 60-70% of PCOS women.

How does thyroid affect my period?

1 in 10 Indian adults has thyroid disease. Hypothyroidism typically causes heavy, prolonged periods; hyperthyroidism causes light or missed periods. Get TSH, free T3, free T4, and anti-TPO tested. Ideal TSH for menstrual regularity is 0.5–2.5 mIU/L. Medication normalizes cycles within 2-3 months.

Why is anemia a concern for Indian women?

57% of Indian women aged 15-49 are anemic (NFHS-5). Heavy periods worsen iron loss. Symptoms: fatigue, pale skin, breathlessness, dizziness. Get CBC + ferritin tested. Iron-rich Indian foods: palak, methi, dates, gur (jaggery), til (sesame), bajra, ragi, rajma. Pair with Vitamin C (amla, lemon) for absorption. Avoid tea/coffee with meals.

What is a normal cycle length?

A normal menstrual cycle is 21-35 days, with an average of 28 days. Cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days may indicate hormonal issues (PCOS, thyroid, stress) and should be evaluated by a doctor.

When should I see a gynecologist?

See a doctor if: (1) Periods missed for 3+ cycles (not pregnant), (2) Bleeding >7 days or soaking a pad every hour for 2+ hours, (3) Severe pain unresponsive to painkillers, (4) Bleeding between periods, (5) Cycles consistently <21 or >35 days, (6) Sudden change in flow, (7) Severe pelvic pain with fever (rule out PID/ectopic).

What is the pre-conception checklist for Indian women?

Key steps: (1) Folic acid 400–500 mcg daily starting 3 months before conception, (2) Anemia screen — take IFA if deficient, (3) Vitamin D check (70-90% deficiency), (4) TSH, HbA1c, blood group, (5) Rubella immunity, (6) Dental checkup, (7) Stop smoking/alcohol, (8) Healthy BMI (Asian cutoffs 18.5–22.9).

What are natural remedies for period cramps?

Evidence-based: heat pads (topical heat), ginger tea (anti-inflammatory), fenugreek (methi) water, ajwain (carom) water, haldi doodh (turmeric milk), omega-3 (walnuts, flaxseeds), magnesium-rich foods (spinach, pumpkin seeds), yoga/light walking. NSAIDs (ibuprofen) are most effective — take at first sign of cramp.

Can I use this for birth control?

No. Calendar-based predictions are NOT a reliable birth control method (12–24% typical-use failure rate). Use proper contraception and consult a gynecologist for family planning.

Medical Disclaimer

This period tracker is for educational and informational purposes only. Cycle predictions are estimates based on the luteal-phase method and may not reflect your actual ovulation or fertile window, especially if you have irregular cycles, PCOS, thyroid conditions, or are perimenopausal.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, contraception, or diagnosis. Do not use this tracker as birth control. For family planning or irregular cycle concerns, consult a gynecologist or reproductive health specialist.

Seek urgent care if you experience: severe pelvic pain with fever, heavy bleeding (soaking pad every hour), fainting, or sudden severe abdominal pain — rule out ectopic pregnancy or PID. India: 112 (national) / 108 (ambulance).
