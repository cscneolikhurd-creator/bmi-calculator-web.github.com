Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this hormone calculator?
Based on Endocrine Society research data and validated lifestyle-hormone correlations. It provides estimates based on population averages — not a clinical diagnosis. Actual hormone levels require blood tests.
What hormones affect men vs women?
Men produce more testosterone (300-1000 ng/dL); women produce more estrogen and progesterone. Both genders have all hormones but in different ratios. Our calculator adjusts for these differences.
How can I improve my hormone health?
Quality sleep (7-9 hours), stress management, strength training 2-3x/week, healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil), reducing alcohol/processed foods, and daily sunlight exposure are the foundations of hormone health.
What does cortisol do?
Cortisol is your primary stress hormone. High cortisol disrupts sleep, increases belly fat, suppresses immune function, and can lower testosterone or estrogen. Chronic stress is harmful. Managing stress is critical for hormone health.
How does age affect hormones?
Testosterone declines ~1% per year after 30. Estrogen drops sharply during menopause (45-55). Cortisol and thyroid can also change with age, stress, and lifestyle. Regular blood tests after 40 are recommended.
Is my hormone data private?
Yes! All calculations happen entirely in your browser. We never store, log, or transmit any of your personal health data to any server. 100% private and anonymous.

Medical Disclaimer

This hormone health calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Hormone levels cannot be accurately determined without a blood test. This tool uses questionnaire-based estimates correlated with lifestyle factors and does not replace clinical blood work (total testosterone, free testosterone, estradiol, cortisol, TSH, T3, T4).

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, endocrinologist consultation, or diagnosis of hormonal disorders (hypothyroidism, PCOS, hypogonadism, Cushing's syndrome, menopause). Do not start, stop, or change hormone therapy based on these results.

If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, hormone levels change dramatically — consult your OB-GYN. If you experience persistent fatigue, unexplained weight changes, mood disorders, or sexual dysfunction, consult an endocrinologist.
