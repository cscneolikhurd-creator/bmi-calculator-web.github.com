Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Indian DRI values differ from USDA?

ICMR-NIN 2020 recommends different values for Indians because: (1) predominantly vegetarian diets with phytate-rich foods that reduce mineral absorption, (2) higher baseline anemia, (3) different body composition and activity patterns, (4) regional dietary habits. For example, Indian iron RDA is 17–21 mg (vs 8–18 mg USDA) and Indian Vitamin A is 1000 mcg (vs 900 mcg USDA).

Why is Vitamin D deficiency so common in India despite sunshine?

Despite abundant sunshine, up to 70–90% of Indians have Vitamin D deficiency. Reasons: (1) darker skin requires more sun exposure for same synthesis, (2) cultural practices covering skin, (3) indoor work culture, (4) pollution blocking UVB, (5) vegetarian diets low in D. ICMR and endocrinology societies recommend 600–800 IU daily, higher than USDA's 600 IU.

Why is B12 a concern for Indian vegetarians?

B12 is naturally found only in animal foods (meat, fish, eggs, dairy). ICMR sets adult RDA at 1 mcg (assuming some dairy), but strict vegetarians and vegans in India are at high risk of deficiency causing anemia and neurological issues. Most experts recommend 2.4+ mcg for vegetarians or fortified foods/supplements. Get B12 tested annually if vegetarian/vegan.

What is the difference between RDA, AI, and UL?

RDA = amount sufficient for 97–98% of healthy people. AI = used when RDA cannot be established. EAR = meets needs of 50% of population. UL = Tolerable Upper Intake Level — highest daily intake unlikely to cause harm. Exceeding UL for fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and minerals (iron, selenium, zinc) can be toxic.

Do I need to hit the DRI every day?

No. DRI targets are averages over time. Aim to hit them most days per week. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) are stored, so occasional shortfalls are fine. Consistent long-term shortfalls are the concern.

Should I take supplements?

Food first is best. Supplements are useful for: Vitamin D in winter/indoor workers, B12 for vegans and strict vegetarians, folate + iron in pregnancy, iron for menstruating women with confirmed deficiency. Get tested before starting high-dose supplements — excess Vitamin A, D, iron, and selenium can be toxic.

What is the anemia prevalence in Indian women?

Per NFHS-5 (2019–21), 57% of Indian women aged 15–49 are anemic — a major public health concern. This is why Indian iron RDA for women is 21 mg (higher than USDA's 18 mg). Pair iron-rich foods (spinach, ragi, jaggery, dates, sesame) with Vitamin C (amla, lemon) to boost absorption. Avoid tea/coffee with meals.

How accurate is this DRI calculator?

Our values follow ICMR-NIN 2020 (India region) or USDA/IOM/NHS (International region). Individual needs vary with genetics, medications, health conditions (diabetes, CKD, thyroid), pregnancy status, and activity. This is general guidance — not personalized medical nutrition therapy. Consult a registered dietitian for medical conditions.
