    Frequently Asked Questions

    What are the healthy WHR cutoffs for Indians?

    For Indians (South Asians), stricter WHR cutoffs are recommended: Women <0.81, Men <0.88 (vs WHO's <0.85/<0.90). This is because South Asians have higher visceral fat at the same WHR and develop diabetes/heart disease 5–10 years earlier than Western populations. Many Indian cardiologists use these stricter cutoffs for risk stratification.

    What is TOFI (Thin Outside Fat Inside)?

    TOFI describes people with normal BMI (<23 for Indians) but high body fat / visceral fat. Up to 30% of normal-BMI Indians have TOFI and metabolic syndrome. It's why BMI alone is unreliable for Indians. WHtR >0.5 with normal BMI suggests TOFI — get a body fat or DEXA scan to confirm.

    What is the South Asian phenotype?

    South Asians (Indians, Pakistanis, Bangladeshis, Sri Lankans) have a distinct body composition: lower muscle mass, higher visceral fat, smaller waist but more dangerous fat distribution, lower HDL, higher triglycerides, and higher insulin resistance. This is why heart disease strikes Indians 5–10 years earlier and at lower BMI.

    What is a healthy WHtR for Indians?

    WHO recommends WHtR <0.5 for everyone ("keep waist less than half your height"). For Indians, some researchers recommend stricter <0.48 to account for higher visceral fat. WHtR is more predictive than BMI for Indians — waist should be under 90cm (men) / 80cm (women) per ICMR.

    How can Indians reduce visceral fat?

    Best evidence: 1) Calorie deficit (300–500 kcal below TDEE), 2) 60 min/day moderate exercise (ICMR 2024), 3) Strength training 2–3×/week, 4) Reduce refined carbs (maida, sugar, white rice), 5) Increase protein (dal, paneer, soya, curd), 6) Add millets (bajra, jowar, ragi), 7) 7–9 hours sleep, 8) Reduce alcohol, 9) Manage stress.

    Why does my WHR say 'moderate risk' but BMI says 'normal'?

    This is a classic TOFI pattern, common in Indians. Your BMI is normal but your belly fat is elevated — which is more dangerous than general obesity. Focus on waist measurement and body fat %, not just BMI. This is why WHR is more useful than BMI for Indians.

    What is a healthy waist circumference for Indians?

    Per ICMR and Asian cutoffs: Men should aim for waist <90cm; Women <80cm. These are lower than Western cutoffs (102cm men / 88cm women) because South Asians have higher metabolic risk at lower waist circumference. Over 90cm (men) or 80cm (women) = high risk, get metabolic screening (glucose, HbA1c, lipids, BP).

    What body shape is healthiest for Indians?

    Pear shape (fat around hips/thighs) is metabolically safer than apple shape (fat around belly). Hourglass is the middle. For Indians, even mild apple shape (waist >80cm women, >90cm men) warrants attention because of South Asian phenotype risk. Exercise + diet can shift fat distribution and reduce apple shape.

    Medical Disclaimer

    This WHR calculator is for educational purposes only. Waist-to-Hip Ratio is a screening tool, not a diagnostic test. Individual health risk depends on many factors including genetics, family history, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and lifestyle. A single measurement should not be used for medical decisions.

    South Asian phenotype note: Indians have higher visceral fat at the same WHR/BMI, so we offer stricter Asian cutoffs (WHR women <0.81, men <0.88; waist <80/90cm; WHtR <0.48). These are based on ICMR and Asian epidemiology.
