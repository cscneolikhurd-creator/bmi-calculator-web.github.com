Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy waist-to-hip ratio?

Per WHO standards: Women should aim for WHR below 0.85, and men below 0.90. Higher ratios indicate increased abdominal fat and higher risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and metabolic syndrome.

What is the difference between WHR, WHtR, ABSI, and BRI?

WHR (Waist-to-Hip Ratio) measures abdominal fat distribution. WHtR (Waist-to-Height Ratio) is a simpler screening tool — should be under 0.5. ABSI (A Body Shape Index) accounts for height and weight. BRI (Body Roundness Index) estimates visceral fat based on waist and height.

What is an apple vs pear body shape?

Apple shape (higher WHR) means fat is stored around the abdomen — higher metabolic risk. Pear shape (lower WHR) means fat is stored around hips and thighs — generally lower health risk. Hourglass is the balanced middle.

How do I measure my waist and hip correctly?

Measure waist at the narrowest point (usually above the navel) after exhaling normally. Measure hips at the widest part of the buttocks. Keep the tape snug but not compressing the skin.

Is WHR better than BMI?

WHR is often better than BMI because it measures fat distribution, not just total weight. Research shows WHR predicts cardiovascular risk more accurately than BMI, especially in athletes or muscular individuals.

Can I lower my WHR?

Yes. Focus on reducing visceral fat through: calorie deficit (300-500 kcal/day), 150+ minutes weekly cardio, strength training 2-3x/week, reducing sugar/alcohol, and 7-9 hours of sleep. Waist typically shrinks before hip.

Medical Disclaimer

This WHR calculator is for educational purposes only. Waist-to-Hip Ratio is a screening tool, not a diagnostic test. Individual health risk depends on many factors including genetics, family history, blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, and lifestyle. A single measurement should not be used for medical decisions.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Consult your healthcare provider for personalized risk assessment and health guidance.
