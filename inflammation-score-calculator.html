Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Dietary Inflammatory Index?

The Dietary Inflammatory Index (DII) was developed by Shivappa et al. (2014) to measure the inflammatory potential of a diet. It scores 45 food parameters from pro-inflammatory (+1) to anti-inflammatory (−1). Higher DII scores correlate with elevated CRP and chronic disease risk.

Can this calculator measure my CRP level?

No. CRP (C-reactive protein) is measured from a blood test. This tool provides an educational estimate of inflammatory risk based on diet and lifestyle factors. Only a blood test can measure actual CRP. If you suspect chronic inflammation, consult a doctor.

What foods are most anti-inflammatory?

Anti-inflammatory foods: fatty fish (omega-3), turmeric, ginger, garlic, leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil, and whole grains. In Indian cooking: haldi, adrak, lehsun, methi, and mustard oil are especially effective.

What causes chronic inflammation?

Common causes: refined oils (high omega-6), excess sugar, refined flour (maida), obesity, smoking, poor sleep, chronic stress, and lack of exercise. Chronic inflammation underlies heart disease, diabetes, and autoimmune conditions.

How long does it take to reduce inflammation?

Dietary changes can reduce inflammatory markers (like CRP) within 4-12 weeks. Significant improvement in symptoms (joint pain, fatigue, brain fog) usually requires 2-3 months of consistent anti-inflammatory eating and lifestyle changes.

Do I need medication to reduce inflammation?

Lifestyle and diet are first-line for chronic low-grade inflammation. Medications (NSAIDs, statins, biologics) are prescribed for specific conditions. Never self-medicate. If you have persistently elevated CRP (>3 mg/L), consult a doctor for evaluation.

Medical Disclaimer

This inflammation score calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. The score is based on a simplified questionnaire inspired by the Dietary Inflammatory Index and does not measure actual CRP or inflammatory biomarkers. CRP, ESR, IL-6, and TNF-α can only be measured through blood tests ordered by a physician.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Chronic inflammation is linked to heart disease, diabetes, autoimmune conditions, and cancer — but lifestyle questionnaires cannot diagnose or rule out these conditions. If you experience persistent joint pain, fatigue, unexplained weight loss, or other concerning symptoms, consult a rheumatologist or internal medicine physician.

Never change medications (NSAIDs, statins, steroids) based on this tool's results. Always consult your healthcare provider before making health decisions.
