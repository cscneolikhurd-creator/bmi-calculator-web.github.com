    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why do Indians have higher inflammation markers?

    Indians have 2-3x higher baseline hsCRP than Western populations due to: (1) higher visceral fat at same BMI (TOFI phenotype), (2) Vitamin D deficiency (70-90%), (3) air pollution (AQI), (4) high refined oil consumption, (5) genetic variants. A "normal" hsCRP of 1-3 mg/L in Indians carries higher cardiovascular risk than in Westerners.

    How much turmeric should I take for inflammation?

    Research shows 500-1000 mg curcumin/day (≈1-2 tsp turmeric powder) can reduce inflammation markers. CRITICAL: always take with black pepper (kali mirch) — piperine increases curcumin absorption by up to 2000%. Best forms: haldi doodh with a pinch of black pepper, or turmeric in dal/sabzi with pepper. Minimum effective dose: 1/2 tsp daily with meals.

    What is a good hsCRP level for Indians?

    Per American Heart Association: Low CV risk <1 mg/L, Average 1-3 mg/L, High risk >3 mg/L. For Indians, aim for <1 mg/L due to higher baseline inflammation. 3-10 mg/L suggests chronic inflammation; >10 mg/L suggests acute infection/inflammation — see a doctor immediately. Test when healthy (not during infection).

    Can air pollution cause inflammation?

    Yes. PM2.5 particles enter the bloodstream and trigger systemic inflammation, raising hsCRP and worsening heart/lung disease. Delhi AQI often exceeds 300 (hazardous). Mitigation: HEPA air purifiers indoors, N95 masks outdoors, avoid morning walks on high AQI days (check AQI before exercise), indoor plants, and Vitamin D supplementation (pollution blocks UVB).

    What blood tests measure inflammation?

    Key inflammation markers: (1) hsCRP — best for chronic low-grade inflammation; (2) ESR — general inflammation; (3) Ferritin — also rises with inflammation; (4) Homocysteine — cardiovascular risk; (5) IL-6 and TNF-α — research markers. Get hsCRP tested when healthy (not during illness).

    What causes chronic inflammation?

    Common causes: refined oils (high omega-6), excess sugar, refined flour (maida), obesity (especially belly fat), smoking, poor sleep, chronic stress, air pollution, Vitamin D deficiency, and lack of exercise. Chronic inflammation underlies heart disease, diabetes, and autoimmune conditions.

    What foods are most anti-inflammatory?

    Anti-inflammatory foods: fatty fish (omega-3), turmeric, ginger, garlic, leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil, and whole grains. In Indian cooking: haldi, adrak, lehsun, methi, and mustard oil are especially effective. Add alsi (flaxseed) and akhrot (walnuts) for plant omega-3.

    How long does it take to reduce inflammation?

    Dietary changes can reduce inflammatory markers (like CRP) within 4-12 weeks. Significant symptom improvement (joint pain, fatigue, brain fog) usually requires 2-3 months of consistent anti-inflammatory eating and lifestyle changes.

    Medical Disclaimer

    This inflammation score calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. The score is based on a simplified questionnaire inspired by the Dietary Inflammatory Index and does not measure actual CRP or inflammatory biomarkers. CRP, ESR, IL-6, and TNF-α can only be measured through blood tests ordered by a physician.

    This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Chronic inflammation is linked to heart disease, diabetes, autoimmune conditions, and cancer — but lifestyle questionnaires cannot diagnose or rule out these conditions. If you experience persistent joint pain, fatigue, unexplained weight loss, or other concerning symptoms, consult a rheumatologist or internal medicine physician.

    Never change medications (NSAIDs, statins, steroids) based on this tool's results. Always consult your healthcare provider before making health decisions.
