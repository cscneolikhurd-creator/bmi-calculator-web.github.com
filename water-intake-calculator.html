Frequently Asked Questions

How much water should Indians drink daily?

ICMR-NIN 2024 recommends ~3L total water for Indian men and ~2.2L for women from all sources (beverages + food). This is slightly lower than IOM (3.7L men, 2.7L women) because of smaller body size. Adjust UP in Indian summers (35–45°C) by 500–800ml, and during high humidity and physical work.

What is ORS and when should Indians use it?

ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution) is a WHO-recommended mix of salt, sugar, and water. Use for: diarrhea, vomiting, heat exhaustion, prolonged heavy sweating, or extreme heat (40°C+). Standard recipe: 1L water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt. Commercial ORS sachets (Electral, Enterogermina) are convenient. Plain water alone can worsen hyponatremia if you're sweating heavily for hours.

How do I test my sweat rate?

Weigh yourself naked before exercise. Exercise 1 hour in typical conditions. Do NOT drink during. Weigh yourself naked after (towel dry). Sweat loss (L) = (pre-weight kg − post-weight kg) × 1 + fluid consumed. Example: 70.0 → 69.2 kg, no drinks = 800ml sweat/hour. Add that to your daily target if you exercise like this regularly.

Can I drink too much water in Indian summer?

Yes — hyponatremia (water intoxication) can happen if you drink >1L/hour without electrolytes. Especially dangerous for marathon runners, endurance cyclists, and outdoor workers in Indian heat. Symptoms: nausea, headache, confusion, seizures. If you're sweating heavily for hours, replace with ORS or lemon-salt-sugar water, not just plain water.

Do Indians need electrolytes more than Westerners?

Yes. Indians lose MORE sodium in sweat than Westerners (~1,000–1,500 mg/L vs 500–800 mg/L) due to genetic adaptation to tropical climate. In Indian summer, add 1/2 tsp salt or ORS to water if sweating >1 hour. Traditional drinks like chaas (buttermilk), nimbu pani with salt, and coconut water are excellent Indian electrolytes.

What is water from food?

About 20–30% of your total water intake comes from food. In Indian diets: dal (90% water), curd (85%), fruits (80–92% — watermelon, cucumber, orange), vegetables (90–96%), rice (70% cooked). This means if you need 3L total, you might only need 2.1–2.4L from beverages.

How does monsoon affect hydration?

In monsoon, humidity reduces sweat evaporation, so you may feel less thirsty but still lose water. Also, monsoon increases water-borne disease risk — drink boiled/filtered water, avoid ice from street vendors, and hydrate with safe sources. Add electrolytes if you sweat in humid weather.

Should I force myself to drink 8 glasses?

No. The 8×8 rule (8 glasses of 8 oz) is a myth with no scientific backing. Your actual need varies by weight, activity, climate, and food. Check urine color — pale straw = hydrated, dark yellow = drink up. Thirst is usually reliable for healthy adults, but older adults (>65) and those in extreme heat lose thirst sensitivity — they should drink on schedule.

Medical Disclaimer

This water intake calculator is for educational purposes only. Hydration needs vary based on genetics, medications, medical conditions (kidney disease, heart failure, liver disease), and pregnancy. Values are population-based estimates.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice. If you have kidney disease, heart failure, liver disease, or take diuretics, consult your physician before changing fluid intake. Do not use this tool's recommendations if your doctor has set a fluid restriction.

If you suspect severe dehydration or water intoxication (hyponatremia), seek emergency medical care immediately. Heat stroke is a medical emergency — call 108 (India) or your local emergency number.
