Frequently Asked Questions

How much water should I drink daily?

The general guideline is 30-35 ml per kg of body weight. For a 70 kg adult, that's about 2.1-2.5 liters of total water per day. Our calculator adjusts for activity level, sweat rate, weather, and lifestyle factors like caffeine and pregnancy.

How does exercise affect water needs?

For every 30 minutes of moderate exercise, add 300-500 ml of water. Heavy sweating requires even more. Athletes should aim to replace 125-150% of sweat losses after exercise.

Does coffee dehydrate you?

Moderate coffee consumption (up to 400 mg caffeine, about 4 cups) does not significantly dehydrate you. However, since caffeine has a mild diuretic effect, adding 200 ml of extra water per cup is a good practice.

What are signs of dehydration?

Common signs: dark yellow urine, thirst, dry mouth, fatigue, dizziness, headache, and reduced urine output. Severe dehydration causes confusion, rapid heartbeat, and fainting — seek medical help immediately.

Does the 8x8 rule (8 glasses) really work?

The 8x8 rule is a rough guideline, not a scientific standard. Actual needs vary by weight, activity, climate, and health. The US Institute of Medicine recommends about 3.7 L total water (men) and 2.7 L (women) from all sources — including food.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes — a condition called hyponatremia (water intoxication) can occur if you drink more than your kidneys can process (about 1 L per hour). Symptoms: nausea, headache, confusion, seizures. Endurance athletes should balance water with electrolytes.

Medical Disclaimer

This water intake calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Hydration needs vary significantly based on individual factors including genetics, medications, medical conditions (kidney disease, heart failure, diabetes insipidus), and pregnancy. The values provided are population-based estimates.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you have kidney disease, heart failure, liver disease, or are on diuretics, consult your physician before changing fluid intake. In hot weather or during intense exercise, monitor for signs of heat exhaustion or hyponatremia.

If you suspect severe dehydration or water intoxication, seek emergency medical care immediately.
