Frequently Asked Questions

What are the three somatotypes?

Endomorph: Larger bone structure, higher body fat tendency, round physique. Mesomorph: Athletic, naturally muscular, medium frame. Ectomorph: Lean, slim, difficulty gaining weight or muscle. Most people are mixed types.

How accurate is the Heath-Carter method?

The Heath-Carter somatotype method has been used since the 1960s and provides reliable classification. It rates body type on three scales (endomorphy, mesomorphy, ectomorphy) from 1-7. Our questionnaire-based version provides strong correlation with anthropometric measurements.

Can I change my somatotype?

Your basic bone structure and somatotype tendencies are largely genetic and don't change significantly. However, you can dramatically change your body composition through diet and exercise. Our AI chat provides personalized recommendations for your body type.

What is the best diet for an endomorph?

Endomorphs do best with a lower-carb, higher-protein diet (40% protein, 30% carbs, 30% fat). Focus on whole foods, portion control, and consider intermittent fasting. Limit refined carbs and sugar.

What is the best workout for a mesomorph?

Mesomorphs respond well to a mix of strength training and cardio. Progressive overload with varied rep ranges (4-6 for strength, 8-12 for hypertrophy) works best. Rotate cutting and bulking phases every 8-12 weeks.

How can an ectomorph gain muscle?

Ectomorphs should eat in a calorie surplus with 5-6 meals daily, prioritize complex carbs and protein (1.6-2.2g/kg), do heavy compound lifts with low volume, limit cardio, and prioritize sleep. Consider creatine supplementation.

Medical Disclaimer

This body shape analyzer uses a questionnaire-based approximation of the Heath-Carter somatotype method. The original Heath-Carter method uses anthropometric measurements (skinfolds, bone widths, circumferences) for classification. Our tool provides educational guidance only.

Somatotype is largely genetic and cannot be changed significantly. However, body composition (fat and muscle) can be improved through diet and exercise. This tool is not a substitute for professional fitness assessment, DEXA scan, or medical advice. Consult a certified trainer or nutritionist for personalized plans.
