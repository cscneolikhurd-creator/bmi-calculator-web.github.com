Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is considered the most accurate formula for calculating Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR). Men: (10 × weight kg) + (6.25 × height cm) − (5 × age) + 5. Women: same but − 161. It's within ±10% accuracy for most people.

What is TDEE vs BMR?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body burns at complete rest — just to stay alive. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is BMR multiplied by an activity factor — the total calories you actually burn per day including exercise and daily movement.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

A safe calorie deficit is 300-500 calories below your TDEE, which typically results in 0.25-0.5 kg (0.5-1 lb) of fat loss per week. Never go below 1200 calories for women or 1500 for men without medical supervision.

What is a good macro split for weight loss?

For weight loss, aim for higher protein: 35-40% protein, 30-35% carbs, 25-30% fat. Protein preserves muscle in a deficit. For muscle gain: 30% protein, 45% carbs, 25% fat. For maintenance: 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat.

Do I need to count calories to lose weight?

Not strictly, but tracking calories helps most people understand their intake and make informed choices. Studies show that tracking improves weight loss outcomes by 30-40%. The calorie deficit is what matters, regardless of tracking method.

How accurate is this calorie calculator?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, accurate within ±10% for most people. Individual metabolism varies based on genetics, muscle mass, hormones, and gut health. Use the results as a starting point and adjust based on real-world weight changes over 2-4 weeks.
