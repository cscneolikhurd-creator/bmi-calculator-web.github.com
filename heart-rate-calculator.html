Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Karvonen formula?

The Karvonen formula calculates target heart rate using Heart Rate Reserve (HRR): Target HR = Resting HR + (Max HR − Resting HR) × Intensity %. It's more accurate than the standard 220-age method because it accounts for your individual resting heart rate and fitness level.

What is the fat-burning heart rate zone?

The fat-burning zone is typically Zone 2 (60-70% of HRR), where your body uses a higher percentage of fat for fuel. However, higher-intensity zones (3-4) burn more total calories, which matters more for weight loss. Total calorie deficit is the real driver of fat loss.

How accurate is the 220-age formula?

The 220-age formula has a standard deviation of ±10-12 bpm — meaning it can be off by 20+ bpm for many people. For accuracy, use the Karvonen method with a measured resting HR, or better yet, get a lab-based max HR test.

What is the best heart rate zone for weight loss?

For weight loss, mix zones: Zone 2 (60-70%) for longer steady-state sessions (30-60 min) builds aerobic base and burns fat efficiently. Zone 3-4 for HIIT burns more total calories and boosts metabolism for hours after. The best zone is the one you'll do consistently.

How do I measure my resting heart rate?

Measure your resting heart rate first thing in the morning, before getting out of bed. Place two fingers on your wrist (radial pulse) or neck (carotid pulse). Count beats for 60 seconds. Do this 3 mornings in a row and average. Normal is 60-100 bpm; athletes often 40-60.

Is it safe to exercise in Zone 5?

Zone 5 (90-100% max HR) is very intense and should only be done in short bursts (30 seconds to 2 minutes). Beginners and people with heart conditions should avoid it. If you feel dizzy, short of breath, or have chest pain, stop immediately and consult a doctor. Get medical clearance first.

Medical Disclaimer

This heart rate zones calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Predicted maximum heart rate formulas (Karvonen, 220-age, Tanaka, Nes) provide estimates — actual max HR can vary by ±10-12 bpm for most people. The most accurate method is a supervised lab test (treadmill or cycle ergometer).

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, cardiac stress testing, or diagnosis of any cardiovascular condition. If you have heart disease, hypertension, arrhythmias, or are on beta-blockers or other heart-rate-altering medications, consult your cardiologist before starting any exercise program.

Stop exercising immediately if you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, fainting, or an irregular heartbeat. Call emergency services if symptoms persist.
