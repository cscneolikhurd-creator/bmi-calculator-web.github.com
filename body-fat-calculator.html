Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

Per ACE (American Council on Exercise):
Men — Essential: 2-5%, Athletes: 6-13%, Fitness: 14-17%, Acceptable: 18-24%, Obese: 25%+
Women — Essential: 10-13%, Athletes: 14-20%, Fitness: 21-24%, Acceptable: 25-31%, Obese: 32%+

How accurate is the US Navy body fat method?

The US Navy circumference method is accurate within ±3-4% compared to DEXA scans, when measurements are taken correctly. It's the most widely used field method for body fat estimation.

What is the difference between US Navy and YMCA methods?

US Navy method uses neck, waist (and hip for women) circumference. YMCA method uses only waist circumference and body weight, making it simpler but less precise. Both are tape measure-based estimates.

How do I measure my neck and waist correctly?

Neck: measure just below the larynx (Adam's apple), keeping the tape slightly angled downward. Waist: measure at the navel level after exhaling normally, keeping the tape parallel to the floor.

What is a good body fat percentage for muscle gain?

For optimal muscle gain, men should aim for 10-15% body fat and women 18-24%. Being too lean (below 8% men, below 16% women) can impair hormone production, recovery, and muscle growth.

Can I reduce body fat without losing muscle?

Yes — through body recomposition: eat 1.6-2.2g protein per kg bodyweight, maintain a small calorie deficit (300-500 kcal), do resistance training 3-4x/week, and prioritize sleep. This preserves muscle while losing fat.

Medical Disclaimer

This body fat calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, which provides estimates within ±3-4% accuracy compared to DEXA scans. Individual results vary based on measurement technique, hydration, and body composition.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, DEXA scan, hydrostatic weighing, or BodPod assessment. Consult a healthcare provider, nutritionist, or certified trainer for personalized body composition goals.
