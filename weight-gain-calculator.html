Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat to gain muscle?

Eat 250-500 calories above your TDEE (maintenance). For lean gains, +250 kcal/day. For faster gains, +500 kcal/day. Higher surpluses (>500) mostly add fat. Aim for 0.25-0.5 kg (0.5-1 lb) gain per week.

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

Research (Helms et al. 2014) shows 1.6-2.2g protein per kg bodyweight is optimal for muscle gain. Higher intakes (up to 3.0g/kg) may benefit advanced lifters but offer diminishing returns. Spread intake evenly across 3-5 meals.

What's the difference between lean bulk and dirty bulk?

Lean bulk: +250 kcal, ~0.25 kg/week gain, mostly muscle with minimal fat. Dirty bulk: +1000 kcal, ~1 kg/week gain, but 50-70% of gain is fat. Lean bulk is recommended for most natural lifters — the fat from dirty bulking is hard to lose.

Can I build muscle in a calorie deficit?

Beginners and those with high body fat can build some muscle in a deficit (body recomposition). Intermediate and advanced lifters generally cannot — they need a surplus. For optimal gains, aim for a slight surplus and adequate protein.

How long does it take to gain muscle?

Natural muscle gain rates: Beginners: 1-1.5 kg/month. Intermediate: 0.5-1 kg/month. Advanced: 0.25-0.5 kg/month. The lean bulk protocol (0.25 kg/week) aligns with intermediate gains — expect visible results in 8-12 weeks.

Should I do cardio while bulking?

Yes, but limit it. 2-3 sessions of 20-30 minutes per week is enough for cardiovascular health without compromising gains. Too much cardio burns calories you need for bulking. Prefer walking, cycling, or swimming over long-distance running.

Medical Disclaimer

This weight gain calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Calorie and macronutrient targets are estimates based on the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and general population averages. Individual needs vary based on genetics, medical conditions, medications, and training patterns.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, sports nutrition consultation, or treatment for eating disorders, metabolic conditions, or hormonal disorders. Consult a registered dietitian or sports medicine physician before starting any aggressive bulking protocol, especially if you have a chronic condition.

Dirty bulking is NOT recommended — the excess fat gained is difficult to lose and negatively impacts health markers (insulin sensitivity, blood lipids, blood pressure). Aim for lean gains with a small surplus of 250-500 kcal/day.

Sources: Mifflin MD et al. (1990) Am J Clin Nutr. | Helms ER et al. (2014) Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation. JISSN. | Slater G & Phillips SM (2011) Nutrition guidelines for strength sports. J Sports Sci.
