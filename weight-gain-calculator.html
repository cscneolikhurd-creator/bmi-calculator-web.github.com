Frequently Asked Questions

How can vegetarians in India gain muscle?

Indian vegetarians have excellent protein sources: paneer (18g/100g), soya chunks (52g/100g dry), dal (9g/cup), rajma, chole, curd, besan, sprouts, tofu. Aim for 1.8–2.2g/kg protein (10–20% higher than meat-eaters due to lower plant protein bioavailability). Combine dal + rice, roti + paneer, or soya + curd for complete amino acid profile.

What's the cheapest protein for Indian bulking?

Budget Indian protein: soya chunks (~₹120/kg for 52g protein), eggs (~₹7/egg for 6g protein), dal (~₹120/kg for 24g protein), peanuts (~₹150/kg for 26g protein), curd (~₹70/kg for 11g protein), besan (~₹100/kg for 22g protein). Soya chunks + eggs + dal combo = 100g protein for ~₹60.

How much protein do Indians need to build muscle?

For Indian lifters: 1.6–2.2g/kg if non-vegetarian. Vegetarians should aim for 1.8–2.4g/kg (plant protein has lower digestibility). For a 70kg lifter, that's 112–154g (meat) or 126–168g (vegetarian) per day. Our calculator adjusts protein up if you select vegetarian or vegan.

What is sattu and why is it good for bulking?

Sattu is roasted gram flour (chana sattu) — a traditional Indian protein drink from Bihar/UP. Per 100g: ~22g protein, ~60g carbs, ~5g fat, ~350 kcal. Sattu + water + lemon + salt = 250 kcal, 15g protein for ₹15. Add banana, milk, ghee = 500 kcal, 20g protein. Excellent budget bulking food.

What are the best Indian bulking foods?

Protein: paneer, soya chunks, dal, rajma, chole, curd, besan, sprouts, eggs, chicken, fish.
Carbs: rice, roti, oats, poha, upma, millets (bajra, jowar, ragi), banana, dates, potato, sweet potato.
Fats: ghee, mustard oil, almonds, walnuts, peanut butter, coconut.
Bulk with: dal-rice + ghee, paneer paratha, soya keema, banana-pb shake, sattu drink.

Can I gain muscle while eating Indian food?

Absolutely. Indian food is muscle-building friendly when structured: dal-rice (complete amino acids), paneer sabzi + roti + curd, egg bhurji + paratha, soya keema + roti, banana-pb shake. Key is hitting protein target (1.6–2.2g/kg) and small calorie surplus (+250–500). Avoid excessive oil, fried foods, and sweets for clean gains.

What's the difference between lean bulk and dirty bulk?

Lean bulk: +250 kcal, ~0.25 kg/week gain, mostly muscle with minimal fat. Dirty bulk: +800 kcal, ~0.8 kg/week gain, but 50–70% of gain is fat. Lean bulk is recommended for most natural lifters — the fat from dirty bulking is hard to lose, and dirty bulking worsens insulin sensitivity, blood lipids, and blood pressure.

How many calories do Indians need to gain muscle?

Use Mifflin-St Jeor BMR × activity factor (1.375–1.725) + 250–500 kcal surplus. For a 65kg Indian adult, this is typically 2,400–2,900 kcal/day. Vegetarians may need 100–200 kcal more because plant proteins have lower digestibility (TEF ~20–25% vs 25–30% for meat).

Medical Disclaimer

This weight gain calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Calorie and macronutrient targets are estimates based on the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and general population averages. Individual needs vary based on genetics, medical conditions, medications, and training patterns.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, sports nutrition consultation, or treatment for eating disorders, metabolic conditions, or hormonal disorders. Consult a registered dietitian or sports medicine physician before starting any aggressive bulking protocol, especially if you have a chronic condition.

Dirty bulking is NOT recommended — the excess fat gained is difficult to lose and negatively impacts health markers (insulin sensitivity, blood lipids, blood pressure). Aim for lean gains with a small surplus of 250–500 kcal/day.
