Frequently Asked Questions

What is a sleep cycle?

A sleep cycle is a ~90-minute pattern your body goes through during sleep, consisting of four stages: N1 (light sleep), N2 (light sleep), N3 (deep sleep), and REM (dreaming). You typically complete 4-6 cycles per night.

Why is waking up mid-cycle bad?

Waking up during deep sleep (N3) causes sleep inertia — grogginess, disorientation, and reduced cognitive performance that can last up to 30 minutes. Timing your alarm to the end of a cycle helps you wake up feeling refreshed.

How much sleep do I need?

Per CDC/NHS guidelines:
• Teens (14-17): 8-10 hours
• Adults (18-64): 7-9 hours
• Seniors (65+): 7-8 hours
Individual needs vary — some adults thrive on 7 hours, others need 9.

What is a chronotype?

A chronotype is your natural preference for sleep and wake times, determined by genetics. Four main types: Lion (early bird), Bear (sun-follower), Wolf (night owl), and Dolphin (light sleeper with irregular patterns).

How do I fix sleep debt?

You cannot fix sleep debt in one night. Add 1-1.5 hours of sleep per night for 3-7 nights until the debt is repaid. Prioritize consistent bedtime, avoid caffeine after 2 PM, and expose yourself to morning sunlight.

Is it better to sleep 6 hours or 7.5 hours?

7.5 hours (5 complete cycles) is far better than 6 hours (4 incomplete cycles with likely mid-deep-sleep wake-up). The difference isn't just duration — it's about completing full 90-minute cycles.

Medical Disclaimer

This sleep cycle calculator is for educational purposes only. Sleep cycle length varies between individuals (typically 70-120 minutes), and the 90-minute figure is an average. Individual sleep needs depend on genetics, age, health conditions, and lifestyle.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you experience chronic insomnia, sleep apnea, excessive daytime sleepiness, or other sleep concerns, consult a sleep specialist or healthcare provider.
