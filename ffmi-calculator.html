Frequently Asked Questions

What is FFMI?

FFMI (Fat-Free Mass Index) measures your muscle mass relative to your height. Unlike BMI, it distinguishes between muscle and fat — making it far more useful for athletes and lifters. FFMI = Lean Mass (kg) / Height² (m²).

What is a good FFMI?

For men: 18-19 average, 20-21 above average, 22-23 excellent, 24-25 exceptional (near natural limit).
For women: 14-15 average, 16-17 above average, 18-19 excellent, 20-22 exceptional.

What is the natural FFMI limit?

Research by Kouri et al. (1995) found that drug-free bodybuilders rarely exceed an adjusted FFMI of 25 (men) or 22 (women). Above these limits, the probability of steroid use becomes very high. Some genetic outliers can reach 26-27 naturally.

How is adjusted FFMI different from raw FFMI?

Raw FFMI favors shorter individuals (they naturally have higher FFMI). The Kouri adjustment normalizes FFMI to a 1.8m (5'11") height, making comparisons fair across heights. Our calculator shows both.

How can I increase my FFMI naturally?

Progressive overload strength training 3-5x/week, eat 1.6-2.2g protein per kg bodyweight, maintain a small calorie surplus (200-400 kcal), sleep 7-9 hours, and be consistent for years. Muscle gain is slow — expect 0.25-0.5 kg per month as an intermediate lifter.

Is FFMI better than BMI?

For athletes and lifters, FFMI is far better. BMI can't distinguish between muscle and fat — a muscular athlete can be classified as obese by BMI. FFMI measures actual muscle mass, making it the preferred metric for physique-focused goals.

Medical Disclaimer

This FFMI calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. FFMI values are estimates based on the Kouri et al. (1995) formula. Individual muscle mass varies significantly based on genetics, training history, nutrition, and hormones.

Natural FFMI limits (25 for men, 22 for women) are statistical estimates from drug-free bodybuilders, not absolute biological limits. Some genetic outliers naturally exceed these values; conversely, some lifters plateau far below. Never use this tool to accuse someone of steroid use — FFMI is not diagnostic.

If you have concerns about muscle mass, hormonal health, or performance, consult a sports medicine physician or registered dietitian.

Sources: Kouri EM et al. (1995) Fat-free mass index in users and nonusers of anabolic-androgenic steroids. Clin J Sport Med. | Schutz Y et al. (2002) Fat-free mass index and fat mass index percentiles. Int J Obes.
