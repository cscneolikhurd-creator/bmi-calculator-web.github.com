Frequently Asked Questions

What is sleep debt?

Sleep debt is the cumulative difference between the sleep you need and what you actually get. Even 1 hour missed per night adds up to a full night's sleep by the end of the week.

How do I recover from sleep debt?

Recover gradually by adding 30-90 minutes of extra sleep daily over 1-2 weeks. Our calculator provides a personalized 7-day recovery plan based on your sleep data.

What is a chronotype?

A chronotype is your body's natural inclination to sleep and wake at certain times. Common types: Morning Lark (early riser), Night Owl (late sleeper), Intermediate (flexible), and Dolphin (light sleeper).

How much sleep do adults need?

Per CDC/NHS guidelines:
• Teens (14-17): 8-10 hours
• Adults (18-64): 7-9 hours
• Seniors (65+): 7-8 hours

Can I catch up on sleep on weekends?

Weekend catch-up sleep helps but doesn't fully repay weekday debt. Research shows it takes several days of consistent 7-9 hours to fully recover. Consistent sleep is better than binge-sleeping.

What are signs of high sleep debt?

Signs include daytime fatigue, irritability, difficulty concentrating, increased appetite (especially for sugar/carbs), weakened immunity, and slower reaction times. Chronic debt is linked to heart disease, diabetes, and depression.

Medical Disclaimer

This sleep debt calculator is for educational purposes only. Sleep needs vary between individuals based on genetics, age, health conditions, and lifestyle. The 7-9 hour guideline is an average for adults — some people thrive on less, others need more.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you experience chronic insomnia, sleep apnea, excessive daytime sleepiness, or other sleep concerns, consult a sleep specialist or healthcare provider.
