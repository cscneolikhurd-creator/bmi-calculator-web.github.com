Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein do Indians need?

For general health: 0.8–1.0g/kg. For muscle gain or fat loss: 1.6–2.2g/kg. Traditional Indian diets are often protein-light — for a 70kg person training, that's 112–154g protein/day. Best Indian sources: paneer (18g/100g), soya chunks (52g/100g), dal (9g/cooked cup), curd (11g/100g), eggs, chicken, fish.

Can vegetarians hit protein targets?

Yes, with planning. Sources: paneer (18g/100g), soya chunks (52g/100g), dal (9g/cup), rajma (13g/cup), curd (11g/100g), Greek yogurt (10g/100g), besan (22g/100g), sprouts (24g/100g). Combine dal+rice, roti+paneer, or soya+curd for complete amino acid profiles. Whey protein helps if needed.

Which diet protocol is best for Indians?

Balanced (30/40/30) works for most. High Protein (40/30/30) is ideal for Indians doing strength training. Mediterranean (25/45/30) has strongest evidence for heart health and diabetes prevention — key for Indians with high diabetes risk. Keto is hard for vegetarians but can work for epilepsy/some metabolic conditions.

What about Indian festivals and social eating?

Festivals (Diwali, weddings) are inevitable. Plan: high-protein breakfast + lunch before the event, moderate portions of favorite dishes, skip mithai if you must choose. One day of overeating won't ruin progress — the other 6 days matter more.

Can I do Zone diet with Indian food?

Yes. Zone is 30/40/30 with 40% carbs (mostly low-GI). Indian version: roti + dal + curd + sabzi (block-balanced), millet roti + paneer + salad, or rice + chicken + ghee. Prioritize low-GI carbs (millets, brown rice) over white rice/maida.

Do I need supplements?

Food first. Consider: whey protein (if hitting 1.6g/kg is hard on Indian veg diet), B12 (strict vegans/vegetarians), Vitamin D (70–90% Indians deficient), omega-3 (if no fish), iron (menstruating vegetarian women). Consult a doctor before starting supplements.

How accurate is the BMR formula?

Mifflin-St Jeor is accurate within ±10% for most people. Individual metabolism varies based on genetics, muscle mass, thyroid function, and medications. Adjust macros based on real-world results over 2-4 weeks.

Do I need to hit my macros exactly?

Aim for ±5g on protein and ±10g on carbs/fats. Consistency over weeks matters more than daily precision. Prioritize protein first, then fill remaining calories with carbs and fats.
