Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Indian Army body fat standard?

Indian Army uses age- and gender-based body fat ceilings via BMI and physical parameters. Typical maximums are approximately 20–26% for males and 30–36% for females, based on the Army's AGI (Adjutant General's Instructions) and medical directives. Confirm with your unit medical officer for official testing.

What is the Indian Navy / IAF standard?

Indian Navy and IAF follow the US Navy circumference method with age-adjusted limits — typically male ~22%, female ~28–33%. Always verify with service medical authorities.

Which BMI cutoff should Indians use?

Indians and South Asians should use WHO Asian cutoffs: Underweight <18.5, Normal 18.5–22.9, Overweight 23–24.9, Obese ≥25. International (Western) cutoffs are 18.5–24.9 normal, 25–29.9 overweight, ≥30 obese.

How does the Army measure body fat?

Most armies, including Indian and US, use the US Navy circumference (tape) method: neck + waist for males, and neck + waist + hip for females.

How accurate is this calculator?

The US Navy method is accurate within ±3–4% compared to DEXA scans. It is the official method used by Indian and US military branches for compliance testing.

Can I use inches instead of centimeters?

Yes. Our calculator supports both metric (cm/kg) and imperial (inches/lbs) units. Toggle the unit selector at the top.

Medical Disclaimer

This calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, which estimates body fat within ±3–4% accuracy compared to DEXA scans. It is not a substitute for official military body composition assessment. Indian force limits shown are approximate and derived from publicly available guidelines. Individual factors (hydration, recent meals, measurement technique) affect results. Always consult your unit fitness leader or medical provider for official compliance testing.
