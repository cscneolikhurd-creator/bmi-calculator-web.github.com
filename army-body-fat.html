Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Army measure body fat?

The US Army uses a tape test measuring neck and waist circumference for males, and neck, waist, and hip circumference for females. The formula is based on the US Navy circumference method.

What is the maximum body fat for the US Army?

For males: 20% (17-20), 22% (21-27), 24% (28-39), 26% (40+). For females: 30% (17-20), 32% (21-27), 34% (28-39), 36% (40+).

How accurate is the Army body fat calculator?

The US Navy circumference method is accurate within ±3-4% compared to DEXA scans. It's the official method used by all US military branches for compliance testing.

What happens if I fail the Army body fat test?

If you exceed standards, you'll be enrolled in the Army Body Composition Program (ABCP) with 6-12 months to meet standards. Failure to comply can result in separation.

Can I use inches instead of centimeters?

Yes. Our calculator supports both metric (cm/kg) and imperial (inches/lbs) units. Just toggle the unit selector at the top right.

Medical Disclaimer

This calculator uses the US Navy circumference method, which provides estimates within ±3-4% accuracy compared to DEXA scans. It is not a substitute for official military body composition assessment. Individual factors (hydration, recent meals, measurement technique) affect results. Always consult with your unit fitness leader or medical provider for official compliance testing.

Sources: US Department of Defense Instruction 1308.3, Army Regulation 600-9, US Navy Body Composition Program, Marine Corps Order 6110.3A, AFI 36-2905.
