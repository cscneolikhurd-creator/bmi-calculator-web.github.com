Frequently Asked Questions

How is the due date calculated?

The due date is calculated using Naegele's rule: add 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP). If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, the calculation adjusts accordingly.

How accurate is the pregnancy due date?

Only about 4% of babies are born exactly on their due date. About 60% are born within one week, and 90% within two weeks of the estimated due date. An early ultrasound (before 14 weeks) is the most accurate method to date a pregnancy.

What are the three trimesters?

Per ACOG: First trimester (weeks 0-13), Second trimester (weeks 14-27), Third trimester (weeks 28-40). Each trimester lasts about 13-14 weeks and has distinct developmental milestones.

How much weight should I gain during pregnancy?

Weight gain recommendations depend on pre-pregnancy BMI:
• Underweight (BMI <18.5): 12.5-18 kg
• Normal weight (BMI 18.5-24.9): 11.5-16 kg
• Overweight (BMI 25-29.9): 7-11.5 kg
• Obese (BMI ≥30): 5-9 kg

When does the baby start moving?

First-time mothers typically feel movement (quickening) between 18-22 weeks, while experienced mothers may feel it as early as 14-16 weeks. Movement becomes regular and noticeable around 24-28 weeks.

When should I time contractions?

Use the 5-1-1 rule: contractions every 5 minutes, lasting 1 minute each, for 1 hour. If this pattern occurs before 37 weeks, seek immediate medical care. After 37 weeks, contact your provider for guidance.

Medical Disclaimer

This pregnancy calculator is for educational purposes only. Due dates are estimates — only about 4% of babies are born on their calculated due date. This tool does not replace professional prenatal care, ultrasound dating, or medical advice. Always consult your OB-GYN, midwife, or healthcare provider for pregnancy-related decisions.

If you experience severe pain, heavy bleeding, reduced fetal movement, severe headache, vision changes, or any concerning symptoms, seek immediate medical attention.
