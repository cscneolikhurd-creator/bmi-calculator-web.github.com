Frequently Asked Questions

What is vascular age?

Vascular age (or heart age) estimates how old your arteries and heart are based on cardiovascular risk factors. If your vascular age is higher than your actual age, your arteries are stiffer than expected for your age — indicating elevated cardiovascular risk.

How is vascular age calculated?

Vascular age is derived from the Framingham 10-year CVD risk score. The calculator finds the age at which a person with ideal risk factors (normal BP, cholesterol, no smoking, no diabetes) would have the same risk as you do now.

What is Pulse Wave Velocity (PWV)?

PWV measures how fast pressure waves travel through your arteries. Higher PWV means stiffer arteries. Normal PWV: <7 m/s (healthy), 7-9 m/s (moderate), >9 m/s (elevated risk). Reference: Reference Values for Arterial Stiffness Collaboration (2010).

Can I lower my vascular age?

Yes. Key steps: control blood pressure (target <130/80), lower LDL cholesterol, quit smoking (greatest single benefit), manage diabetes, exercise 150+ minutes weekly, eat a Mediterranean or DASH diet, and maintain healthy weight.

How accurate is this vascular age calculator?

This is an educational estimate based on the Framingham risk model. Actual vascular age requires measuring arterial stiffness directly (carotid-femoral PWV, pulse wave analysis). Use as a motivational tool, not a diagnosis.

When should I see a cardiologist?

See a cardiologist if your 10-year CVD risk is 20% or higher, you have a family history of early heart disease, or you experience chest pain, shortness of breath, or heart palpitations. Get an annual checkup if you have diabetes or hypertension.

Medical Disclaimer

This vascular age calculator is for educational and motivational purposes only. Vascular age is an estimate based on the Framingham Heart Study risk model and simplified for use in this tool. It is not a clinical measurement of arterial stiffness.

Actual vascular age requires direct measurement of arterial stiffness (carotid-femoral pulse wave velocity, pulse wave analysis, or augmentation index). This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, cardiac evaluation, or treatment decisions.

Do not change medications or treatment based on this tool's results. If you have cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, high cholesterol, family history), consult a cardiologist for proper assessment.

If you experience chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or heart palpitations, call emergency services immediately.
