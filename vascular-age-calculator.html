Frequently Asked Questions

Why are South Asians at higher heart risk?

South Asians (Indians, Pakistanis, Bangladeshis, Sri Lankans) develop heart disease 5–10 years earlier than Western populations and have 2–4× higher risk at the same BMI. This is due to the "South Asian phenotype": higher visceral fat, lower muscle mass, higher insulin resistance, and elevated Lp(a). Standard Framingham/PCE equations under-estimate South Asian risk by 30–40%. This calculator offers optional ×1.4 calibration.

What are CSI cardiovascular risk thresholds for Indians?

The Cardiological Society of India (CSI) 2023 guidelines recommend treating 10-year ASCVD risk ≥10% as "high risk" warranting statin therapy. This is more aggressive than AHA/ACC (which treats ≥20% as high, 7.5–20% as intermediate). CSI's stricter threshold reflects South Asian baseline risk.

What BP target should Indians aim for?

Per ICS (Indian College of Cardiology) 2023 and CSI, Indians should aim for BP <130/80 mmHg. This is stricter than the older <140/90 target, reflecting evidence that South Asians benefit from lower BP targets due to higher stroke and coronary disease burden.

What is a heart-healthy Indian diet?

DASH + Indian adaptation: whole grains (bajra, jowar, ragi, brown rice, whole wheat roti), 2–3 servings dal/legumes daily, low-fat curd, paneer in moderation, nuts (almonds, walnuts), seeds (flax, chia), 5+ servings vegetables, 2 fruits. Use mustard/rice bran oil (not deep frying), limit ghee to 1 tsp/meal, <5g salt/day, avoid fried snacks, sweets, refined flour (maida), and sugary drinks.

What is TOFI?

TOFI = "Thin Outside, Fat Inside". It describes people with normal BMI but high visceral fat and metabolic risk. Common in South Asians — up to 30% of normal-BMI Indians have metabolic syndrome. Waist-to-height ratio (<0.5 healthy) and body fat % are better indicators than BMI alone.

How can I lower my vascular age?

Biggest impact: quit smoking (reduces vascular age by up to 10 years in 1 year), lower BP to <130/80, LDL cholesterol to <100 mg/dL (<70 if high risk), HbA1c <7%, exercise 60 min/day (ICMR), Mediterranean/DASH-Indian diet, weight loss 5–10%, manage stress, sleep 7–9 hours.

Is this calculator clinically validated?

This is a heuristic estimate inspired by the Framingham Heart Study, NOT a validated clinical tool. True vascular age requires direct measurement of arterial stiffness (carotid-femoral PWV, pulse wave analysis). Use this as a motivational and educational tool, not for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

What is a normal PWV?

Pulse Wave Velocity (PWV) measures arterial stiffness. <7 m/s healthy, 7–9 m/s moderate, >9 m/s elevated risk. However, PWV naturally increases with age and BP — a 65-year-old will have higher PWV than a 30-year-old even at good health. Interpretation should be age-adjusted.

Medical Disclaimer

This vascular age calculator is a heuristic estimate inspired by the Framingham Heart Study and adjusted for South Asian risk. It is not a validated clinical tool, not equivalent to an actual Framingham risk score, and NOT a substitute for direct measurement of arterial stiffness (carotid-femoral PWV, pulse wave analysis).

Actual vascular age requires direct arterial stiffness measurement. This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, cardiac evaluation, or treatment decisions. Do not change medications or treatment based on this tool's results.

If you have cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, high cholesterol, family history), consult a cardiologist for proper assessment. If you experience chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or heart palpitations, call emergency services immediately.
