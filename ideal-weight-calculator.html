Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Peterson formula for ideal weight?

The Peterson formula (2016) calculates ideal weight based on height:
Men: 50 kg + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4)
Women: 45.5 kg + 0.91 × (height in cm − 152.4)
It's considered more accurate than older formulas like Devine or Robinson for modern populations.

What is a healthy weight for my height?

A healthy weight is typically defined as a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9. For height 170 cm (5'7"), that's roughly 53.5–72 kg (118–159 lbs). However, ideal weight varies based on muscle mass, body frame, age, and sex.

Does body frame affect ideal weight?

Yes. People with a larger frame can carry more weight healthily. Body frame is estimated by wrist circumference:
Men: small <16.5 cm, medium 16.5–19 cm, large >19 cm
Women: small <14 cm, medium 14–16.5 cm, large >16.5 cm

Is ideal weight the same as healthy weight?

Not exactly. Ideal weight is a single number derived from a formula. Healthy weight is a range (BMI 18.5–24.9). Aiming for a healthy range is more realistic and forgiving than hitting an exact number, which can vary by ±5 kg and still be healthy.

Is BMI the best measure of healthy weight?

BMI is a good population-level screening tool but has limitations — it doesn't distinguish muscle from fat, doesn't account for fat distribution, and varies by ethnicity. For a fuller picture, combine BMI with waist circumference, body fat %, and fitness markers.

How long does it take to reach ideal weight?

At a safe rate of 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week, losing 10 kg takes about 20 weeks. Faster loss (more than 1 kg/week) risks muscle loss, nutrient deficiencies, and rebound weight gain. Aim for sustainable changes rather than crash dieting.

Medical Disclaimer

This ideal weight calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Ideal weight formulas provide population-level estimates and do not account for individual factors such as muscle mass, bone density, body composition, age, ethnicity, or medical conditions.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Athletes, pregnant women, elderly individuals, and people with metabolic conditions (diabetes, thyroid disorders, PCOS) may have healthy weights outside the standard ranges. Consult a registered dietitian or physician for personalized weight goals.

If you are experiencing unintended weight loss/gain, an eating disorder, or body image concerns, please reach out to a healthcare provider or a mental health professional.
