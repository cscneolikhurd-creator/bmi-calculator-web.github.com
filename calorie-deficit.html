Frequently Asked Questions

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit is when you consume fewer calories than your body burns. A deficit of 500 calories per day typically results in 0.5 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week, since 1 kg of fat is approximately 7,700 calories.

What is a safe calorie deficit?

A safe deficit is 300-750 calories per day (0.25-0.75 kg/week). Deficits above 1000 calories per day are not recommended without medical supervision, as they can lead to muscle loss, nutrient deficiencies, and metabolic adaptation.

How long does it take to lose weight?

At a safe deficit of 500 calories/day, you'll lose approximately 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week. For 10 kg (22 lbs), it takes roughly 20 weeks (5 months). Rapid initial weight loss is mostly water, not fat.

What should I eat in a calorie deficit?

Prioritize high-protein foods (1.6-2.2g/kg bodyweight), fiber-rich vegetables, whole grains, and healthy fats. Protein preserves muscle, fiber keeps you full, and whole foods are more satiating than processed foods at the same calories.

Can I lose weight without counting calories?

Yes, but tracking helps. Studies show calorie tracking improves weight loss by 30-40%. If you don't want to track, focus on portion control, eating protein with every meal, and avoiding liquid calories.

Why am I not losing weight in a deficit?

Common reasons: (1) underreporting calories (studies show people underestimate by 20-50%), (2) water retention masking fat loss, (3) metabolic adaptation after long dieting, (4) thyroid or hormonal issues, (5) medications. Track your weight weekly, not daily.

Medical Disclaimer

This calorie deficit calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Weight loss rates and calorie targets are estimates. Individual results vary based on genetics, metabolic health, medications, and adherence.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, registered dietitian consultation, or treatment for eating disorders, diabetes, thyroid conditions, or other metabolic disorders. Do not consume fewer than 1200 calories/day (women) or 1500 (men) without medical supervision.

If you have a history of eating disorders, please consult a healthcare provider before starting any diet plan.
