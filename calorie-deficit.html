Frequently Asked Questions

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit is when you consume fewer calories than your body burns. A deficit of 500 calories per day typically results in ~0.45 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week, since 1 kg of fat is approximately 7,700 calories.

What is a safe calorie deficit?

A safe deficit is 300-750 calories per day (0.25-0.75 kg/week). Deficits above 1000 calories/day are not recommended without medical supervision.

How many calories should Indians eat to lose weight?

For most Indian adults, safe deficit means 1400-1800 kcal/day for women and 1600-2200 kcal/day for men. Never go below 1200 kcal (women) or 1500 kcal (men) without medical supervision.

What are good Indian protein sources?

Vegetarian: paneer (18g/100g), soya chunks (52g/100g), dal (9g/cup), rajma, chole, curd (11g/100g), Greek yogurt.
Eggetarian: + eggs (6g each).
Non-veg: + chicken breast (31g/100g), fish (22g/100g), mutton (25g/100g).

What is a good Indian meal plan for weight loss?

Example 1600 kcal veg day: Breakfast — 2 moong dal chilla + curd; Snack — 100g paneer + green tea; Lunch — 2 roti + dal + paneer sabzi + salad; Snack — buttermilk + roasted chana; Dinner — paneer bhurji + 1 millet roti + sabzi. Protein ~85g, Carbs ~140g, Fat ~55g.

How much protein do I need per kg?

General health: 0.8-1.0 g/kg. Weight loss / muscle gain: 1.6-2.2 g/kg. For a 70kg person training for fat loss, that's 112-154g/day. Traditional Indian diets are often carb-heavy and protein-light — this is the #1 gap.

How long does it take to lose 10 kg?

At a safe deficit of 500 calories/day, you'll lose ~0.45 kg/week. For 10 kg, that's roughly 22 weeks (~5 months). Rapid initial weight loss is mostly water, not fat.

Why am I not losing weight in a deficit?

Common reasons: (1) underreporting calories (people underestimate by 20-50%), (2) water retention masking fat loss, (3) metabolic adaptation after long dieting, (4) thyroid or hormonal issues, (5) medications. Track weight weekly, not daily.

Medical Disclaimer

This calorie deficit calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Weight loss rates and calorie targets are estimates. Individual results vary based on genetics, metabolic health, medications, and adherence.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, registered dietitian consultation, or treatment for eating disorders, diabetes, thyroid conditions, or other metabolic disorders. Do not consume fewer than 1200 calories/day (women) or 1500 (men) without medical supervision.

If you have a history of eating disorders, please consult a healthcare provider before starting any diet plan.
