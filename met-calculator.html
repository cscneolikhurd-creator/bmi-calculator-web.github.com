Frequently Asked Questions

What is a MET value?

MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) measures the energy cost of physical activity relative to rest. 1 MET = resting metabolic rate (about 1 kcal/kg/hour). Walking at 5 km/h is ~4.3 METs, running at 10 km/h is ~10 METs.

How accurate is the MET calorie formula?

The MET formula (Calories = MET × 3.5 × weight kg / 200 × minutes) is accurate within ±10-15% for most people. It doesn't account for individual metabolic efficiency, fitness level, or movement patterns. Use it as a guideline, not gospel.

What is EPOC afterburn?

EPOC (Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption) is the extra calories your body burns after exercise to recover. High-intensity activities (HIIT, heavy lifting) produce more EPOC — typically 6-15% of total calories, sometimes up to 25% for beginners.

Does intensity affect MET values?

Yes. Faster running has higher METs than jogging. Our calculator applies intensity multipliers (0.8x light, 1.0x moderate, 1.2x vigorous, 1.4x max) as a simplified adjustment. For precision, choose the correct activity speed.

How many calories should I burn per workout?

There's no universal target. For fat loss, aim for 300-500 calories per session, 3-5 times per week. For general health, 150+ minutes of moderate activity weekly (per CDC/NHS guidelines). Focus on consistency, not burning maximum calories.

Why is my calorie burn different from my fitness tracker?

Fitness trackers use heart rate + accelerometer data, which can be off by 10-40%. MET-based calculations are population averages. Neither is exact. Use trends (not exact numbers) to guide your fitness plan, and adjust based on real-world results.

Medical Disclaimer

This MET activity calculator is for educational and informational purposes only. Calorie burn estimates are based on population averages from the Compendium of Physical Activities (Ainsworth et al.) and are accurate within ±10-15% for most people. Actual calorie burn varies based on individual metabolic rate, fitness level, movement efficiency, and environmental factors.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, cardiac stress testing, or personalized fitness assessment. If you have heart disease, hypertension, diabetes, joint problems, or are pregnant, consult your physician before starting or modifying any exercise program.

Stop exercising immediately if you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or fainting. Call emergency services if symptoms persist.
