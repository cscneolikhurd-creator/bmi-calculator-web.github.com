Frequently Asked Questions

What is carb cycling?

Carb cycling is a nutrition strategy where you alternate between high-carb, low-carb, and no-carb (rest) days throughout the week. High-carb days fuel intense workouts and replenish glycogen; low-carb days enhance fat oxidation; rest days promote recovery.

How many high and low carb days per week?

Common protocols:
• Standard: 3 high, 2 low, 2 rest
• Aggressive Cut: 2 high, 3 low, 2 rest
• Maintenance: 4 high, 2 low, 1 rest
• Keto Cycle: 1 high, 2 low, 4 rest
Align high-carb days with your hardest workouts.

Is carb cycling better than a standard diet?

Research shows carb cycling and standard calorie restriction produce similar fat loss when calories and protein are matched. Carb cycling may offer psychological benefits, better workout performance on high days, and improved adherence for some people. It's not required for fat loss.

What should I eat on low carb days?

Focus on protein (chicken, fish, eggs), healthy fats (avocado, olive oil, nuts), and leafy greens. Limit starchy carbs to small portions (berries, small sweet potato). Aim for 1.8-2.2g protein per kg bodyweight to preserve muscle.

Can I do carb cycling as a vegetarian?

Yes. High-carb days: beans, lentils, quinoa, oats, fruits. Low-carb days: tofu, tempeh, seitan, paneer, Greek yogurt, nuts, seeds, olive oil, leafy greens. Protein target stays the same (1.6-2.2g/kg) from plant sources.

How long until carb cycling shows results?

Fat loss results typically show in 2-4 weeks. Initial weight loss in week 1 is mostly water (glycogen depletion). Muscle preservation and performance improvements may appear within 3-6 weeks. Consistency matters more than the protocol itself.

Medical Disclaimer

This carb cycling planner is for educational and informational purposes only. Carb cycling is a nutrition strategy — not a medical treatment. Results vary based on genetics, metabolic health, training intensity, and adherence.

This tool is not a substitute for professional medical advice, registered dietitian consultation, or treatment for diabetes, thyroid conditions, PCOS, or eating disorders.
